\subsection{Generalisation of the GRU Hidden State Beyond the Training Horizon}

A notable and somewhat surprising finding from the streaming MISRGRU evaluation is that
reconstruction quality --- measured by RSP --- continued to improve as the number of
frames accumulated at inference time grew from 20 to 100, well beyond the chunk sizes
used during truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT).
During training, gradients were prevented from flowing across chunk boundaries, so the
model was never directly optimised for dependencies longer than \texttt{chunk\_size} frames.
Yet at inference time the GRU hidden state is never reset, and the model continues to
benefit from accumulating additional context.

This suggests that the ConvGRU hidden state acts as a compressed, continuously updated
summary of blinking statistics that generalises beyond the temporal horizon it was trained on.
The explanation is consistent with how GRUs are known to operate: the update and reset gates
learn to selectively retain or discard information, and a well-trained gating mechanism can
maintain useful long-term running statistics even when the network never received a training
signal to do so explicitly.
In the SOFI context, more frames directly provide more independent realisations of the
fluorophore blinking process, and the GRU implicitly integrates this additional evidence
at each step via its hidden state, even beyond the supervised horizon.

Practically, this means the streaming model can be deployed with a longer warm-up window
than it was trained on, trading slightly higher initial latency for improved reconstruction
fidelity once enough frames have been processed.
It also motivates future work on explicitly training with longer TBPTT windows or with
a curriculum that gradually increases the chunk length, which may unlock further gains
in the long-frame regime.

\subsection{Noise-Prior Fine-Tuning for Microscope Adaptation}

A promising direction for adapting a synthetically trained model to a specific
microscope is to fine-tune the encoder on a short sequence of blank frames —
frames acquired with the laser on but no sample present, capturing only
detector noise and autofluorescence.
Since the correct SR output for such frames is zero, the model can be
penalised for any non-zero prediction, forcing the encoder to recalibrate
its noise threshold to the actual instrument.
This one-shot update, requiring no labelled data and no additional
experimental effort, could be applied as a pre-processing step immediately
before reconstruction.
This is related to test-time adaptation~\cite{wang2021tent}, but with an
unambiguous supervision signal: any model activation on a blank frame is
by definition a false positive.
